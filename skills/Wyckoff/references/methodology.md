# Structural interpretation

## Scope and evidence

Wyckoff supplies a vocabulary for interpreting price-volume structure. The vocabulary does not establish predictive profitability. The Composite Operator is an interpretive device; a chart cannot identify the participants or prove a coordinated campaign. Use "consistent with absorption" rather than "institutions are definitely buying."

The three traditional laws are analytical ideas: supply/demand, cause/effect and effort/result. In the point-and-figure (P&F) tradition, horizontal counts estimate objectives. Range duration alone is not a P&F count, and no count guarantees a target.

## Context and range boundaries

- Establish the prior trend and the analyzed timeframe before choosing an accumulation/distribution hypothesis. Reaccumulation and redistribution are continuation interpretations, not synonyms for any pause.
- Anchor the initial accumulation range to observed stopping action and the automatic rally; use the selling-climax area and rally high as provisional boundaries, refined by subsequent tests. Mirror the reasoning for a buying climax and automatic reaction in distribution.
- Record exact boundary observations and any subsequent revision. A dense closing-price area may be a useful internal zone, but is not automatically the full trading range.
- Separate an internal range, a local shakeout and the larger structure. Do not move the boundary after seeing a break solely to preserve the label.
- For intraday comparisons, consider time-of-day volume patterns. For relative strength, compare aligned asset and benchmark observations; relative outperformance can coexist with absolute losses.

## Phases are context, not a compulsory sequence to draw

| Phase | Accumulation interpretation | Distribution interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| A | Prior decline shows stopping action | Prior advance shows stopping action |
| B | Range develops; repeated tests explore supply/demand | Range develops; repeated tests explore supply/demand |
| C | Supply test, possibly a spring/shakeout or a higher-low test | Demand test, possibly an upthrust/UTAD or a lower-high test |
| D | Strength and supportive reactions become visible | Weakness and failing rallies become visible |
| E | Price operates beyond the range in a developing uptrend | Price operates beyond the range in a developing downtrend |

Not every chart exposes Phase A, every event, or an identifiable phase. Phase C need not undercut support or exceed resistance. Preserve `unknown` when classification exceeds the evidence; distinguish absent events from events outside the visible history.

## Event interpretation

| Event | Observations to examine | Limits and failure evidence |
|---|---|---|
| PS / PSY | Preliminary support/supply during the preceding trend | May recur; does not establish the final extreme |
| SC / BC | Selling/buying climax candidate in context, with spread, close and activity evidence | One high-volume bar is insufficient; news and continuing trend can produce similar bars |
| AR | Automatic rally after selling pressure, or automatic reaction after buying pressure | Always spell out which meaning applies |
| ST | Secondary test relative to prior stopping action | Compare displacement, spread and activity; a local test alone cannot establish the whole phase |
| Spring / shakeout | Excursion below established support followed by recovery; inspect rejection and subsequent tests | A continuing breakdown is not a successful spring. High-volume shakeouts may need a subsequent test; low volume is not a universal first-bar requirement |
| UT / UTAD | Excursion above resistance followed by rejection; UTAD requires the later distribution context | Not every failed breakout is UTAD; sustained acceptance above resistance contradicts rejection |
| SOS / SOW | Directional progress, quality of closes, spread and activity relative to the range | One line cross is insufficient; define what progress/acceptance means for this particular read |
| LPS / LPSY | A supportive reaction or weak rally consistent with an emerging imbalance | Can occur without a textbook spring/UTAD; LPS/LPSY are not always near the extreme |

Use `minor sign of strength`, `major sign of strength`, and their weakness counterparts in full when the distinction matters. Do not collapse them into a single token or force all variants into Phase B.

The typical protective stops below support belong to long positions; protective stops above resistance belong to shorts. Breakout entry orders may also be present. A candle does not reveal the actual order inventory or prove intentional stop hunting.

## Effort and result

Compare activity with price displacement, bar spread, closing location and follow-through in context. High volume with little progress may reflect absorption, two-sided disagreement, an auction or an event-driven transfer. Reduced volume on an advance may reflect weak demand or reduced supply. Neither relation supplies a standalone direction rule.

Use comparable baselines whose observations end by the cutoff. If a numeric detector is requested, specify its exact windows, thresholds and confirmation rules as project choices, not original Wyckoff laws. Do not invent such thresholds merely to give discretionary labels a quantitative appearance.

The traditional nine buying/selling tests are a checklist, not nine equally weighted predictors. If using them, consult the source table in [sources.md](sources.md); mark unassessed tests explicitly. Do not rename a custom checklist "the original nine tests" or map a count directly to a win rate or position size.

## Conditional planning

Phase C entries generally involve earlier interpretation and greater uncertainty; Phase D confirmation and Phase E continuation have different entry conditions. Do not declare LPS the only valid entry or SOS never an entry. State which setup is being considered and why.

Tie invalidation to that setup. A spring-based long may use loss of the spring low; an LPS-based thesis may use the relevant reaction low. State the trigger convention, any buffer and the cost/gap assumptions. Do not place a generic stop at "range low minus 1%" when that contradicts the actual spring extreme. Mirror the logic for shorts.

For targets, distinguish observed resistance/support, measured projections and P&F estimates. A P&F objective requires the chart construction, box size, reversal setting, count line, count extent and counting convention. If these are absent, leave the P&F target unassessed. Do not substitute an arbitrary multiple or the number of days in the range.

## Illustrative decisions (synthetic, not performance evidence)

- A range resolves higher, with a supportive higher low and later strength but no undercut: a no-spring interpretation remains possible. Do not invent a spring to complete Phase C.
- Price undercuts 100 to 98 and closes at 101, then later trades below 98 under the stated invalidation rule: preserve the earlier recovery observation, mark the spring-based thesis failed and reassess. Distribution remains a separate hypothesis.
- Price and volume permit equally plausible continuation and reversal reads: use `WAIT` and identify the observation that would distinguish them; do not issue "75% accumulation."
